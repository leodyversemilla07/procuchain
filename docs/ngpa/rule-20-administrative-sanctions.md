# Rule XX - Administrative Sanctions

## Sections 92-97

This rule covers the administrative sanctions for violations of procurement laws and regulations.

---

## Section 92. Administrative Liabilities of Public Officers

### 92.1 Acts Constituting Violations
The following acts of public officers shall constitute **administrative violations**:

- a) Approving or awarding a contract without prior recommendation of the BAC
- b) Splitting of contracts to avoid competitive bidding
- c) Opening of bids before the scheduled time
- d) Delaying the procurement process without justifiable cause
- e) Approving procurement without the required documents
- f) Awarding contract to an ineligible bidder
- g) Failure to post bid opportunities and award notices in PhilGEPS
- h) Failure to observe procurement procedures
- i) Collusion with bidders
- j) Other acts analogous to the above

### 92.2 Penalties
Public officers found liable for administrative violations shall be subject to:
- Dismissal from service
- Suspension
- Fine
- Disqualification from holding public office
- Other penalties as provided by law

---

## Section 93. Private Sector Participation

### 93.1 Private Sector Violations
Private sector participants may be held liable for:
- Submission of false documents
- Misrepresentation
- Collusion
- Fraud
- Corruption
- Other violations of procurement laws

### 93.2 Sanctions
Private sector violators may be subject to:
- Disqualification from bidding
- Forfeiture of bid security
- Blacklisting
- Civil and criminal prosecution
- Other penalties as provided by law

---

## Section 94. Civil Liability

### 94.1 Liability for Damages
Any person or entity causing damage to the government through violation of procurement laws shall be:
- Liable for damages
- Subject to the payment of costs
- Liable for consequential damages

### 94.2 Solidary Liability
Where two or more persons are liable, they shall be **solidarily liable** for damages.

---

## Section 95. Criminal Liability

### 95.1 Criminal Acts
The following acts shall be criminally punishable:

- a) **Fraud in Procurement:** Using fraudulent documents or misrepresentations
- b) **Collusion:** Conspiracy to manipulate procurement outcomes
- c) **Corruption:** Offering or accepting bribes
- d) **Interference:** Undue interference in procurement proceedings
- e) **Bid-Rigging:** Agreement among bidders to fix prices or allocate contracts
- f) **Other violations** as defined by law

### 95.2 Penalties
Criminal violations shall be punishable by:
- Imprisonment
- Fines
- Perpetual disqualification from public office
- Forfeiture of benefits
- Other penalties as provided by the Revised Penal Code and special laws

---

## Section 96. Grounds for Imposition of Administrative Sanctions

### 96.1 Grounds for Sanctions Against Procuring Entities
Administrative sanctions may be imposed on Procuring Entities for:

- a) Non-compliance with procurement procedures
- b) Failure to post procurement opportunities
- c) Delays in procurement without justification
- d) Failure to submit required reports to GPPB
- e) Splitting of contracts
- f) Non-observance of transparency requirements

### 96.2 Grounds for Sanctions Against Bidders/Contractors
Administrative sanctions may be imposed on bidders/contractors for:

- a) Submission of false eligibility documents
- b) Withdrawal of bid after bid opening without valid cause
- c) Failure to sign the contract without valid cause
- d) Failure to furnish required securities
- e) Violation of contract terms
- f) Abandonment of contract

---

## Section 97. Imposition of Administrative Sanctions

### 97.1 Authority to Impose Sanctions
Administrative sanctions shall be imposed by:
- The HoPE for violations within the Procuring Entity
- The GPPB for violations affecting the entire procurement system
- Other authorities as provided by law

### 97.2 Due Process
Before the imposition of sanctions:
- The respondent shall be given notice
- An opportunity to be heard shall be provided
- Investigation shall be conducted
- Decision shall be in writing

### 97.3 Appeals
Decisions on administrative sanctions may be appealed to:
- The Civil Service Commission (for government employees)
- The appropriate courts (for private individuals/entities)
